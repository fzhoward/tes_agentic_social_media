# **Google Business Profile Post Skill \- Portable V1**

*For local service business standalone GBP posts*

# **Purpose**

Generate clear, useful Google Business Profile posts for local service businesses. This is a portable writing skill. It contains no brand, industry, platform, scheduling, or automation assumptions. Supply the business context, service area, topic, audience, desired action, and any firsthand experience before generating posts.

# **Core Principle**

A Google Business Profile reader is usually in research or decision mode. They are not scrolling passively. They found the business through local search, maps, or the business profile itself. The post should help them quickly decide whether the business understands their situation and can help them take the next step.

A strong GBP post does three things:

1. Names the customer situation, problem, decision, event, offer, or trust signal.  
2. Gives a compact answer or reason to care.  
3. Points toward one clear next action.

The post should not feel like a generic advertisement. It should feel like a useful, local, specific message from a business that understands the customer's real situation.

# **Scope**

## **This Skill Produces**

* One standalone Google Business Profile post per topic.  
* A body written to follow a separately generated hook.  
* A CTA line matched to the post goal.  
* Post pattern selection for review and iteration.  
* JSON-only structured output.  
* Quality notes and validation flags.

## **This Skill Does Not Produce**

* Opening hooks. Hooks are assumed to be generated by a separate hook skill.  
* Images, image prompts, or creative assets.  
* Scheduling logic, API calls, platform IDs, or publishing instructions.  
* Blog posts, email campaigns, or social media posts for other platforms.  
* Brand strategy, offer design, or service pricing.

# **Input Quality Rule**

Before generating a post, evaluate whether the inputs are specific enough. Inputs are thin when they lack all three of the following:

* A specific customer, project, problem, or situation.  
* A concrete local, seasonal, service, product, or job condition.  
* A real reason, lesson, opinion, offer detail, proof point, or next step.

When inputs are thin:

* Use only supported facts.  
* Avoid invented anecdotes, outcomes, claims, or local details.  
* Flag the issue in quality\_notes.input\_quality.  
* Keep the post useful but more general.  
* Do not create fake specificity to make the post sound stronger.

# **Post Pattern Set**

Select one pattern based on the post goal and available information. Keep this set small. Add new patterns only when a recurring GBP use case clearly does not fit.

## **1\. Condensed Answer**

Use when the post answers a common local customer question or explains a service-related issue.

* Name the customer situation or question.  
* Give the compact answer in 2-3 short sentences.  
* Add one specific local, service, or experience detail if available.  
* Close with one clear CTA.

## **2\. Decision Guide**

Use when the customer is comparing choices or deciding what service, package, product, timing, or provider fits their situation.

* Name the decision.  
* Give the 2-3 main factors that determine the right choice.  
* Explain what the business can help them clarify.  
* Close with one clear CTA.

## **3\. Situation-Specific**

Use for seasonal, weather, local condition, event timing, permit, deadline, availability, or urgency-driven situations.

* Name the specific situation.  
* State what matters right now.  
* Connect the situation to the customer's next step.  
* Close with one clear CTA.

## **4\. Offer / CTA**

Use for a specific offer, promotion, availability window, limited service slot, new service push, or direct booking prompt.

* Name the customer need or situation first.  
* State the offer clearly without hype.  
* Include key limits, dates, or conditions.  
* Close with the direct action.

## **5\. Trust / Proof**

Use when the goal is to build confidence through reviews, completed work, guarantees, policies, certifications, local presence, or customer outcomes.

* Lead with the customer concern or decision risk.  
* Present the proof point plainly.  
* Explain why it matters to the customer.  
* Close with a low-friction next action.

## **6\. Event / Announcement**

Use for business updates, new services, new location details, schedule changes, community support, local events, hiring, or operational announcements.

* State the update in plain language.  
* Explain who it affects or helps.  
* Include date, time, location, or availability if relevant.  
* Close with the next step.

# **Hook Handling**

This skill assumes a separate hook skill creates the opening hook. The GBP post body generated here must not include a competing hook. It should begin with body content that naturally follows the hook.

The final assembled post should follow this structure:

\[Hook from hook skill\]

\[GBP post body from this skill\]

\[CTA line if not already included in body\]

The body must not repeat the hook, resolve the hook too abruptly, or introduce an unrelated angle.

# **Formatting Rules**

* JSON output only.  
* Plain text post body only.  
* No markdown formatting.  
* No bold, italic, headers, or bullet symbols in the post body.  
* No emojis.  
* No exclamation points.  
* No em dashes. Use commas, periods, or simple hyphens instead.  
* Every content line followed by a blank line for readability.  
* Target 80-120 words by default, adjustable only when the input requests a different range.  
* Target median sentence length: 10-14 words.  
* Maximum sentence length: 20 words.  
* Reading level: roughly 7th-8th grade.  
* One clear CTA.  
* One core point per post.
* **Never include a phone number or street address in the GBP post body.** Google rejects posts that do (phone-stuffing content policy). The phone number is delivered by the CALL button using the verified listing number; the address is shown on the listing. State call-to-action intent in words (e.g. "Call for availability") WITHOUT typing the number.

# **Voice and Content Rules**

* Lead with the customer's situation, problem, decision, offer relevance, proof concern, or local context.  
* Do not open with a credential statement about the business.  
* Do not make the business the hero. The customer's job or need is the focus.  
* Use first person only if the brand voice input calls for it.  
* Do not position the business as the cheapest option unless that is explicitly true and strategically desired.  
* Avoid corporate marketing language and generic claims.  
* Avoid unsupported superlatives such as best, top-rated, number one, guaranteed, or easiest unless the input provides proof and the claim is compliant.  
* Do not make complex, risky, regulated, or skilled work sound overly simple or risk-free.  
* Use local references only when they add real meaning.  
* Name the service, problem, condition, offer, event, or proof point when known.  
* Do not invent customer stories, statistics, reviews, certifications, guarantees, or job outcomes.

# **CTA Rules**

The CTA should match the post goal. Keep it direct and low-friction.

| Post Goal | CTA Style | Examples |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Answer / education | Read or learn more | Read the full breakdown. Learn what to check before you schedule. |
| Lead generation | Call, message, request quote, book | Call for availability. Request a quote. Message us with your project details. |
| Offer | Claim, book, ask about availability | Ask about this offer. Book before the date listed. |
| Trust / proof | Low-friction contact | Call with your questions. Message us before you decide. |
| Event / announcement | Attend, call, check details | Stop by during event hours. Call before you come by. |

# **Quality Checklist**

* Does the body assume a separate hook will be prepended?  
* Does the body avoid writing its own opening hook?  
* Does the post lead with the customer's situation instead of a business credential?  
* Does it match one of the defined post patterns?  
* Does it have one core point?  
* Is the CTA clear and matched to the goal?  
* Is the word count within the requested or default range?  
* Does every sentence stay under 20 words?  
* Is the post free of markdown, emojis, exclamation points, and em dashes?  
* Are all claims supported by the provided inputs?  
* Would a local searcher in decision mode find it useful and specific?

# **Implementation Notes**

* This skill stops at content generation.  
* Publishing platform, scheduling, image attachment, and API logic belong in a separate implementation document.  
* If a hook skill is used upstream, the final assembly step should prepend the selected hook with a blank line before the GBP body.  
* If the hook skill fails, do not ask this GBP skill to generate a hook unless the implementation explicitly overrides the default.  
* If required CTA destination is missing, generate the post only when the desired action can still be stated without a link or phone number. Flag the missing destination in quality notes.  
* When the desired action on GBP is a phone call, the CTA is realized by the CALL button (not by a number in the body). The button dials the verified listing number, so no phone digits ever appear in the post text.  
* If required offer, event, or proof details are missing for those post types, select a different pattern or flag the post as failing.
