T.E.S. Rentals Brand Voice Skill
A consolidated brand voice and content rules document synthesized from the uploaded content frameworks.
Portable Skill Document
Purpose
Keep T.E.S. Rentals content consistent across blogs, email, Facebook, Instagram, Google Business Profile, hooks, image overlays, and formatted website content.
Brand Position
* Premium independent equipment rental company serving North Florida.
* Practical local operator, not a national chain and not a bargain-bin rental shop.
* The value is correct machine fit, uptime, well-maintained equipment, transparent guidance, and better job outcomes.
* The customer’s job is the protagonist. T.E.S. Rentals is the guide.
Core Voice
* Practical
* Plainspoken
* Customer-facing
* Locally aware when relevant
* Confident without exaggeration
* Helpful without sounding corporate
* Specific and grounded in real jobs
* Direct, but not pushy
What T.E.S. Should Not Sound Like
* A national rental chain
* A marketing agency
* A motivational influencer
* A hype-heavy sales page
* The cheapest option in town
* A business making equipment operation sound easy, risk-free, or simple for everyone
Default Content Rules
* Use first-person voice from Zeb when content is educational, advisory, or experience-based.
* Lead with the customer’s project, problem, decision, or risk.
* Never open with “T.E.S. Rentals is...” unless context requires it.
* Never position T.E.S. Rentals as the cheapest option.
* Never write generic content that could apply to any rental company anywhere.
* Use geographic references only when they add real meaning.
* Use specific machines, job types, soil/ground conditions, counties, towns, and project constraints when relevant.
* Do not fabricate stories, customer examples, or field experience.
Experience Integration
* Zeb’s experience is the differentiator.
* Weave experience into the content rather than quoting it mechanically.
* Use real job details: machine, condition, county, mistake, outcome, or lesson.
* If experience input is thin, flag it and lean on facts instead of inventing specificity.
* A strong experience detail can support blog sections, hooks, social posts, email story fragments, and GBP specificity.
Specificity Standard
Weak: Soil conditions matter.
Strong: Wet clay in Bradford County can change whether a compact excavator is the right choice.
Weak: We offer quality equipment.
Strong: If the job is tight, muddy, or root-heavy, machine fit matters more than just rental price.
Formatting Tone Defaults
* No exclamation points in standard educational content.
* No emojis unless a specific social concept intentionally uses them.
* No em dashes.
* Short, readable sentences.
* Blank lines for social and email readability.
* No markdown in platform copy where markdown renders as literal characters.
* Avoid dense paragraphs on social and email.

Verifiable claims only
Every specific factual claim must trace back either to the catalog data provided or to general common knowledge.
* No invented customer stories, testimonials, or anecdotes. "Last month a customer..." is off-limits unless that story is in the inputs.
* No invented dollar amounts, hourly rates, or price ranges. Use a value only if it's in the catalog.
* No invented local statistics — counts of subdivisions, permits, projects, demographics. General framing ("North Florida's building season", "Clay County clay") is fine; specific numbers about the area are not.
* When in doubt, write general framing instead of a specific number.


Channel-Specific Voice Rules
Channel
	Voice Job
	Key Rule
	Blog
	Answer clearly, then teach with local and field context.
	First sentence of exec summary should be direct and citable.
	FAQ
	Give self-contained answers that can be cited alone.
	Direct answer first. No “as mentioned above.”
	Email
	Create tension and drive to the blog.
	Do not give away the full how-to.
	FB/IG Link Post
	Create unresolved tension.
	CTA points to first comment or link location.
	FB/IG Advisory Post
	Give one practical takeaway.
	No link required. Different angle from link post.
	GBP
	Condense the answer for someone already searching.
	Lead with the job/problem, not credentials.
	Hooks
	Attention plus implied payoff.
	Pain/cost/problem framing should dominate, but not become manipulative.
	Image Overlay
	Stop the scroll in a few words.
	3-7 words, one idea, no clutter.
	

Trust and Safety Rules
* Never make a machine sound safer or easier than it is.
* Avoid guaranteed outcomes.
* Do not imply every customer can operate equipment well without experience.
* Be honest about variables: ground conditions, access, roots, slope, weather, project scope, and operator skill.
* When the right answer depends on job details, say so clearly.
Standard CTAs
* Equipment fit: “Not sure which machine fits your job? Call us at (904) 452-0888. Tell us what you’re working on and we’ll help you figure out the right equipment.”
* Cost/budgeting: “Want to talk through what your job might cost to rent for? Call us at (904) 452-0888 or fill out our project inquiry form at tesrents.com.”
* Timing/planning: “Ready to get on the schedule? Call us at (904) 452-0888 or check availability at tesrents.com.”
* Regulatory/permit: “Questions about what you need before starting your project? Call us at (904) 452-0888. We’ve walked a lot of customers through this.”
* General: “Questions about which equipment fits your project? Call us at (904) 452-0888 or fill out our project inquiry form at tesrents.com.”
Brand Voice Quality Checklist
* Does this sound like Zeb giving practical advice?
* Is the customer’s job at the center?
* Is there a specific machine, condition, job type, location, or decision where useful?
* Did we avoid cheap-price positioning?
* Did we avoid hype and corporate language?
* Did we avoid oversimplifying risky equipment work?
* Could a competitor paste their name into this and use it unchanged? If yes, make it more specific.
